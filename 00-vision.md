# Linea, the full product vision

Written 3 October 2026. This is where Linea goes if everything works.
Nothing here is built on build day. It exists so that every teammate
answers "where does this go" the same way, and so the Q and A has a
floor under it.

## The one sentence

Linea is a voice-first, fully automated welfare check system purpose-built
for older adults.

Tagline: **Keeping families connected, one LINEA at a time**

Pronounced **lin-ya**, from Filipino "linya", meaning "line".

The product stays with Filipino elders every day on the phone they already
have and brings family into the conversation when it matters.

## The idea underneath it

The Philippines is becoming an aging society while its children work in
other cities and other countries. The elder is at home with a keypad
phone. The daughter is in Dubai with a smartphone. Every product built
for this family so far has been built for the daughter's device, and so
it never reaches the elder. Linea is built for the elder's device, which
is a phone call, and it treats the daughter's device as the door into
that call. That inversion is the whole company.

## What Linea becomes, in phases

### Phase 1. The morning call, for families

The product we build this week, made real.

-   A daily call at the elder's time, in her language, asking about
    sleep, medicine and how she feels, and listening.
-   Alerts to the family with her exact words, and the family joining
    the live call from anywhere, briefed by Linea.
-   A calendar of green, yellow and red days, with summaries and
    transcripts.
-   An evening call for evening medicine, and a number the elder can
    call herself when she wants company or help, which rings Linea first
    and her family second.
-   The intro recorded in the family's own voice, and consent recorded
    in the elder's own voice.

Who pays: the adult child, per elder per month, priced against a few
minutes of calling a day. Channels: remittance apps and counters,
recruitment agencies and pre departure seminars, OFW communities, the
Department of Migrant Workers. The north star metric is mornings
answered.

### Phase 2. The quiet health record, for the health system

After ninety days Linea knows an elder's normal, which nobody else does.

-   Trend detection over weeks. A month of missed morning medicine, a
    week of poor sleep, a mood that has been low for ten days, surfaced
    to the family and, with consent, to the barangay health worker.
-   Weekly summaries for barangay health workers and rural health units,
    so a BHW with two hundred elders knows which five to visit this
    week.
-   Post discharge follow up for hospitals and HMOs, where the same
    morning call checks on a patient sent home with instructions nobody
    read.
-   Medicine refill reminders tied to the pharmacy, and a voice order
    the family approves, so lola never runs out of Losartan again.
-   An escalation path where the RHU nurse or doctor joins the call the
    same way the daughter does, briefed by Linea, when the family asks
    for it.

Who pays: LGUs per barangay for their senior citizen and hypertension
programs, HMOs and hospitals per patient per month for follow up, and
families still. The metric is hospital visits avoided and adherence
sustained.

### Phase 3. The voice layer for a family across distance

Linea stops being a check-in and becomes the thing a separated family
talks through.

-   A local trunk partner in the Philippines so calls cost pesos, not
    dollars, and the number lola sees is a Philippine number.
-   Bisaya, Ilonggo, Waray, Ilocano and Bikol, so Linea speaks the
    language of the kitchen, not the capital.
-   Change detection in the voice itself. Speech that slows over months,
    breath that shortens, words that go missing, flagged carefully,
    never diagnosed, and handed to the family and the doctor as a reason
    to look. This is only possible for whoever has the daily record, and
    only Linea will.
-   The scam shield. Lola asks Linea about a call she just got, and
    Linea, which is the one number she trusts, tells the family and
    tells her to wait. The number of elders defrauded by phone falls
    because the trusted voice is already in the house.
-   Grandchildren in the call. The apo in Manila reads a story at
    bedtime through the same door the daughter uses, with Linea keeping
    the pace and helping with the hard word.
-   A Linea speaker for elders with no phone at all, a small device that
    rings, listens and talks, built on Agora's device kit, so the last
    elder in the barangay is reached too.
-   The same product in Indonesia, Vietnam and India, where elders stay
    home and children work abroad, with the Philippine playbook as the
    template.

Who pays: families, LGUs, HMOs, remittance partners who bundle Linea
with a monthly transfer, and insurers who would rather pay for a morning
call than for a hospital stay. The metric is the number of families
where the morning call is simply part of the day.

## What Linea will never do

These are written now so the vision cannot drift into them later.

-   Never give medical advice or interpret symptoms. The record is for
    people to act on.
-   Never pretend to be a person or a family member.
-   Never sell or share the record. Data moves only with specific
    consent, to people the family chose.
-   Never carry advertising.
-   Never replace the family's own calls. Every alert is an invitation
    to join.

## The moat, one line each

-   The joined call is structural. Competitors are bots talking to the
    elder alone or humans calling for the family; Linea puts both in one
    room.
-   The record accumulates. Every elder on Linea is a longer history
    than any new entrant can build.
-   The culture is a playbook. Honorifics, Taglish, consent in her own
    voice, the daughter's intro. Global products start from zero on all
    of it.
-   The OFW channel is first mover. The buyer is concentrated in
    channels no eldercare company uses.
-   Trust compounds. Lola knows the voice and the family trusts the
    calendar, and both take months to earn.

## The numbers behind it

9.2 million Filipinos were 60 and over in 2020, projected to about 13
million by 2030. 12 percent of older Filipinos live alone and one in ten
of their households has a member abroad. 2.19 million Filipinos worked
overseas in 2024. Half of older persons' households have no internet. 67
percent of their healthcare is paid out of pocket. Sources in
`../evidence/sources.md`.

## How the vision answers the finals rubric

-   Impact and significance: an aging society reached on the device it
    already has.
-   Deployment feasibility: a phone number and a browser, no hardware,
    no integration, consent and privacy designed in.
-   Scalability: every OFW family, then every barangay, then every
    country with the same family shape; a local trunk brings cost to
    pesos.
-   Innovation: the joined call and the daily record, which no bot and
    no human service has together.
-   Defense: everything above is written down and can be said in one
    breath.
